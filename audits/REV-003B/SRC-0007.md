## Reconstruction

The modeled decision problem is `⟨G,P,R,U⟩`: `G={g1,g2}` is the questioner's contextual goal; `P∈Δ(G)` is the respondent's uncertainty; `R={r_exh,r_ms1,r_ms2}` contains direct answers; and `U:G×R→R` assigns payoff (p. 5, Computational Model). Conditions vary uncertainty `ε∈{ε_H,ε_L}` and exhaustive-answer cost `δ∈{δ_L,δ_S}` (p. 5, payoff table). A soft-max policy chooses among direct responses: `π(r)=SoftMax(α EU(r))`, where `EU(r)=Σ_g P(g)U(g,r)` (p. 5). Clarification probability is `P(r_cq)=Logistic(τ(ExpRegret(r*)−c))`, with `r*=argmax_r π(r)`, `ExpRegret(r*)=Σ_g P(g)Regret(g,r*)`, and regret equal to the best state-conditioned utility minus the chosen action's utility (p. 5). Thus clarification is a separate first-layer choice; otherwise the actor follows the direct-response policy.

Experiment 1 is a within-subject `2×2` forced-choice study (`N=125`) manipulating uncertainty about the questioner's goal (cover-story category preferences) and response cost (two versus four options per category). The dependent variable is selection among a clarification question, exhaustive answer, or either category-specific mention-some answer (pp. 2–3, Figure 1). Clarification increased under high uncertainty particularly with a large option space; exhaustive answers increased under high uncertainty in the small space; uncertainty and space had credible effects on clarification, and the reported interaction contrasts supported the preregistered qualitative pattern (p. 3).

Experiment 2 is a `2×3` likelihood-rating study (`N=120`, two excluded) manipulating wrong-action cost and no/low/high uncertainty, with underdetermination in reference, manner, or deadline. Ratings (0–100) cover one to three direct actions, a clarification question, a non-clarification question, verbal acceptance, and inaction (pp. 3–4, Figure 2). Clarification rose from no to nonzero uncertainty but not from low to high; direct-action ratings declined gradiently; higher error cost increased clarification and decreased direct action (p. 4). The authors explicitly leave utility specification across linguistic and non-linguistic actions, explicit asking costs, free response production, and richer social contexts to future work; the fitted Experiment 1 model misses mention-some response bias and some exhaustive-answer behavior (pp. 5–6, Discussion).

## D. Boundary verdict

1. **Demonstrated mechanism:** a fitted expected-regret account of uncertainty- and cost-sensitive ask-versus-answer choice for Experiment 1, plus two human studies showing related choice/rating patterns for questions and directives.
2. **PCC coverage:** strongest for PCC-C01, PCC-C06, and PCC-C07; clarification/repair partly informs PCC-C08.
3. **Unlocated or out of scope:** persistent multi-actor public state, public acceptance and promotion, organizational authority, work-artifact/workflow state, a consequence-bearing *shared-state* transition, and participant contestation of an inspectable diagnostic.
4. **Necessity to PCC:** those residual links—not expected regret alone—are necessary to PCC's proposed public-warrant, governed-state, and contestable-record combination.
5. **Verdict:** **partial overlap (high confidence)**. This is important inherited machinery for action-sensitive ask-versus-act decisions, but it neither implements nor evaluates PCC-style public warrant or promotion.
